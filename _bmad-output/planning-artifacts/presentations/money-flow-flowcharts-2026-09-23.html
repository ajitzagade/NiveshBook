✅
Confirmed — different projects can have entirely different partners

A partner's share is a row in partner_shares, keyed to one project_id — never a global relationship. This is already coded (not just planned): the table has no cross-project link at all. Project A's roster and Project B's roster can overlap fully, partially, or not at all, each at its own percentage. Demonstrated live in the interactive demo (Project A vs. Project B).

🔎
Newly confirmed — the same person can be a main partner in one project and a sub-partner in another

Checked directly against packages/db/src/schema.ts: partner_shares.partner_id and subpartner_shares.sub_partner_id are freestanding IDs with no foreign key to a shared "person" table and no exclusivity constraint between them. Nothing in the schema or the core authorization logic ties one identity to only ever being a "main partner" or only ever a "sub-partner."

This is exactly the pattern your four-project sheet uses — Sandip Z is a main partner (50%) in Project A, but a sub-partner under Chandrakant Z in Project B. That's not something anyone had to specifically build for it to work; it falls out of shares being per-project records, not per-person roles. It isn't a written, tested scenario yet though — worth adding as an explicit acceptance criterion once account-to-share linking is built.

⚠️
A real conflict — how a sub-partner's percentage is measured

Your sketches compute a sub-partner's rupee amount as a percentage of their parent partner's share. Example from your own notes: Partner B holds 50% of the project; SubPB2 is entered as 60%, and gets ₹30,000 out of Partner B's ₹50,000 (60% of ₹50,000).

The database schema already built for this (Story 2.3) does the opposite on purpose — a code comment states the sub-partner's percentage is "always a percentage of the full Project... never a fraction of the parent Partner's own share." Under that rule, to land on the identical ₹30,000, SubPB2 would need to be entered as 30% (of the whole project), not 60%.

Both rules can produce the split you want — they just require typing a different number into the same field. Concretely, for Partner B's ₹50,000 (50% of the project):

This needs an explicit decision before Story 2.3 (already in review) goes further: if the current schema stays, sub-partner percentages need to always be entered as their eventual share of the whole project, not of their parent's slice.
